## 2. Narrative by jurisdiction

### NIST (United States standards body)
The three core standards, FIPS 203, 204 and 205, were published on 13 August 2024 and remain the foundation; NIST's own project page (updated 2026-08-05) tells implementers to use them now. FIPS 203 and 204 carry published errata lists that will be folded into future revisions, so implementers should track them. HQC was selected on 11 March 2025 as a backup KEM but, as of the project page date, has no FIPS ("coming soon"). FN-DSA (Falcon) is the key moving item: as of 28 September 2026 NIST staff were still consulting on how to write the draft FIPS 206 (fixed-point arithmetic, KAT-exact validation), so no draft was published as of today; a final FIPS 206 is further away. For embedded work this matters because Falcon-class small signatures are attractive but its signing procedure is the hard part. SP 800-208 (LMS/XMSS, October 2020) is unchanged on its page; it names firmware authentication for long-lived constrained devices as the intended use, but restricts key and signature generation to FIPS 140 Level 3 hardware modules, which is a design constraint for on-board signing. A new draft SP 800-230 (April 2026) proposes SLH-DSA variants limited to 2^24 signatures per key for firmware and software signing; it is a draft and its final form is uncertain. NIST IR 8547 is still an initial public draft from November 2024 on the CSRC page: it proposes deprecating 112-bit quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms after 2030 and disallowing all of them after 2035. Treat those dates as proposed, not final.

### United States government
Retrieved: NSM-10 (4 May 2022; goal to mitigate as much quantum risk as feasible by 2035) and OMB M-23-02 (18 Nov 2022; prioritized cryptographic inventory). NOT retrieved: NSA CNSA 2.0 documents and CISA pages (Access Denied). I therefore make no claim here about CNSA 2.0 algorithms, dates or LMS/XMSS firmware-signing guidance; the user should fetch the CNSA 2.0 advisory and FAQ directly (see Gaps).

### United Kingdom (NCSC)
Version 1.0 of the migration timelines, published 20 March 2025, sets three milestones: 2028 (discovery and plan), 2031 (highest-priority migration done, plan refined), 2035 (complete). It calls out resource-constrained, hard-to-upgrade industrial IoT devices and mentions LMS/XMSS secure boot already offered by some vendors. Some of its forward-looking statements (IETF standards around 2027, hardware acceleration 2026-27, first FIPS 140-3 modules in 2025) predate today and should be re-checked.

### European Union
The EU PQC Workstream roadmap (Part 1 v1.1, dated 11 June 2025) asks Member States to have national strategies and First Steps by end of 2026, finish high-risk migrations by end of 2030, and do as much as feasible by 2035. It recommends hybrid solutions where feasible, and says products with a market life beyond 2030 should be upgradable to PQC with a post-quantum-capable firmware update signature path. That is the single most relevant government statement for drone and robot makers found so far. ENISA: only a general topic page was retrieved, no dated deliverable. Germany (BSI): PQC by default, hybrid preferred, TR-02102 version 2026-01, with hybrid TLS groups from RFC 10024 to be added to the next TR-02102-2. France (ANSSI): hybrid recommended; do not buy non-PQC products after 2030; a crypto-agility paper dated 19 January 2026 exists but was not read. Netherlands: no page retrieved (see Gaps).

### Other jurisdictions
Canada: federal timeline April 2026 plan, 2031 high-priority, 2035 remaining (June 2025 document). Australia: stop traditional asymmetric cryptography by end of 2030 and does not recommend hybrids (via WebFetch summary of a page updated 22 Sep 2025). Singapore: handbook and readiness index issued for consultation in Oct 2025; final status unknown. Japan: interim report (Nov 2025) targets about 2035 for government systems, with a roadmap still to come.
