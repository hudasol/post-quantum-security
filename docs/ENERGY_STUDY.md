# Energy and CPU headroom study

Author: Huda Mueen. Generated by `energy/run_all.py` (run `python3 -m energy.run_all` from the repository root). Seed 20261020. Do not edit by hand.

## Labels

Cycle counts are `published` (a row of `data/evidence.csv`, cited by id) or `derived from published` (a published time multiplied by the stated clock). Energy per operation is `estimated`: cycles multiplied by a cited run current per MHz and a voltage. Mission shares are `modelled` from labelled assumptions and have no validation data; no validation error can be reported for them. Where a core has no published row the Cortex-M4 row is used and the label says `proxy`.

### Check against published energy

Rows S2-0190 to S2-0213 (NUCLEO-F411RE, 96 MHz) carry published cycles and published energy (24 rows). Estimating their energy with the cited STM32L4R5 LDO figure (110 uA/MHz) and 3.3 V gives a published over estimated ratio of 1.70 to 2.25; median absolute error without calibration 54.8 percent (bootstrap 95 percent interval 54.2 to 55.1). After calibrating one scale factor (2.21) on a seeded half (seed 20261020, split in `energy/results/validation_split.csv`), the median absolute error on the 12 validation rows is 0.7 percent (interval 0.4 to 1.7, maximum 27.8). The low power part figure therefore underestimates a general purpose Cortex-M4 board by about the calibration factor; the proxy factor range of 1 to 3 used in section 4 covers it. The published energies are board level and the F411 is not a cited part, so this is a check of the method's order of magnitude only. The small calibrated error mostly reflects that the published energies are close to a constant board power times run time; the largest errors are on the elliptic curve rows, whose stated average power is lower. The uncalibrated energies in section 2 should be read with this error beside them.

## 6. Findings, including null results

- Energy is not the binding constraint in this model: at the nominal workload the ML-DSA-44 design uses 0.089 percent of the Crazyflie battery energy per hour against 0.16 percent for the Ed25519 baseline (modelled). CPU time is the tighter budget, and it is driven by the command rate.
- On Cortex-M4, ML-DSA-44 verification takes 0.555 times the cycles of the Ed25519 verify row used here, so post quantum verification is not the slower operation in this comparison. Other multiples: ML-DSA-87 1.638, LMS 1.039, SLH-DSA-128s (pre standard SPHINCS+ row) 2.919, XMSS 5.106; ML-DSA-44 signing 3.624 times Ed25519 signing. The classical figure depends strongly on the library chosen (section 2).
- No published HMAC cycle counts exist in the evidence table; the symmetric session design is estimated only.
- The energy method was checked against 24 published energy rows on one Cortex-M4 board; the uncalibrated estimate is low by a factor near 2.2. No energy check exists for the Cortex-M33, Cortex-M7 or RISC-V parts, and the mission shares have no validation data.
