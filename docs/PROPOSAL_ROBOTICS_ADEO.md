# Proposal summary for ADEO: post quantum ready command and update channels for drones and robots

Author: Huda Mueen. Two page summary of `docs/PROPOSAL_ROBOTICS.md`, generated by `scripts/build_paper.py`; every number is read from a result file.

This is a student research proposal and a seed for discussion. It is not a deployment, it has no relationship with or endorsement by any organisation named, and organisation facts come only from the public pages listed in `docs/CONTEXT_ENTITIES.md`, cited by their id.

## Problem

Drones and robots authenticate commands and firmware on microcontroller flight computers over narrow links. The post quantum replacements are standardised, but their cost on such devices and links, and which devices must be replaced rather than updated, are not yet measured.

## Why this may be relevant (public facts only)

Public facts that may make this work relevant are recorded, with their sources, only in `docs/CONTEXT_ENTITIES.md`: ADEO-01, ADEO-02, ADEO-03, ADEO-04. None of them implies any relationship with or endorsement by the organisation.

ADEO publishes little on technology under its own name; the items above that do not name ADEO are Abu Dhabi government statements, as `docs/CONTEXT_ENTITIES.md` notes.

## What already exists

- 1,530 published rows with provenance, every new row read by two independent readers.
- Flight stack memory from 10 real builds (host measured): default PX4 builds leave 6.0 to 45.9 kB of flash free, and the fleet's median hardware replacement share rises from 0.201 to 0.300 once measured memory replaces the assumption (estimated).
- ns-3 explains the 802.15.4 gap by sufficient causes: one hop takes 5.72 s in ns-3 against a published 30 to 60 s, and 3 hops, 16 loaded neighbours or a cited backoff setting each bring it into range (simulated (network)).
- Formal verification of the session and update design: 39 of 57 queries proved, and design findings F1 to F3 (vehicle authentication, replay after a power cycle, firmware downgrade) fixed and proved (modelled).
- A cycle model with a median held out error of 1.0 percent within one code base, but 9.8 percent when a whole scheme is left out (modelled), and 36 board predictions for a caches off pass registered before any board session.
- A mission simulation: with a hybrid session, command availability on a telemetry radio stays at 0.991; a signature on every command lowers it to 0.794 (estimated).

## Proposed next step

A one afternoon board session and a hardware in the loop study with a borrowed flight controller and telemetry radio, scored against predictions registered in this repository, followed by a reference design note for a session based command and update channel (`docs/REFERENCE_ARCHITECTURE.md`).
