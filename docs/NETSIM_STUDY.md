Transfer protocol: application level selective repeat over UDP, the closest ns-3 equivalent of `linksim/sim.py` selective_repeat: the sender sends a window of 16 segments, the last one asks for a block acknowledgement with a bitmap, a timeout resends the last segment as a poll, and unacknowledged segments return to the pending list. The timeout is 2 times the smoothed round trip of a window (linksim derives it from known air times instead) and doubles after each expiry until a new sample (timer details in the table). MAC acknowledgements and retries run underneath, as in a real stack, while linksim counts one MAC acknowledgement per frame without retries.

### Gap verdict

At one hop with ns-3 defaults the transfer takes 5.72 s in ns-3 (simulated (network)), against 7.12 s in linksim (modelled) and 30 to 60 s in the paper (published); ns-3 is -80.9% from the low end of the published range.

Single causes that bring the ns-3 time into the published range: hops = 3 (ns-3 mean 55.20 s, 95% interval 51.81 to 58.59 s; hop count is an ASSUMPTION (the paper gives none)); neighbours = 16 (ns-3 mean 33.21 s, 95% interval 18.98 to 47.44 s; background load is an ASSUMPTION (the paper gives none)); macMinBE = 8 (ns-3 mean 40.10 s, 95% interval 37.95 to 42.24 s; inside the cited range (N1.1, N2.1) with macMaxBE 8 (N1.2)). Each is sufficient in simulation; the paper states none of these settings (N6.1), so the simulation shows which causes can explain the gap, not which one applied in the paper. Causes tied to a cited setting: macMinBE; the others rest on ASSUMPTIONS.

Combined cells in the range (listed above) keep every CSMA parameter inside its cited range; their hop counts, spacing and loads are ASSUMPTIONS.

Multi hop forwarding in ns-3 costs more than the store and forward ASSUMPTION of linksim: at 2 hops ns-3 26.41 s against linksim 14.25 s; at 3 hops ns-3 55.20 s against linksim 21.37 s. The window of segments is forwarded while the sender keeps sending on the same channel, so frames of different hops contend and collide (MAC drops in `netsim/results/lrwpan_cells.csv`), which linksim does not model.

Pre set rows: 4 of 5 inside their 95 percent interval. At least one miss among 5 independent 95 percent intervals has probability 0.23 by chance; the follow up rows (ten more independent seed blocks at the rate that missed, added after the first run and labelled as such) have a pooled mean inside its interval.

Rows marked studies as run use the simulation as the protocol and mission studies do, with acknowledgements lost at the same rate. Then the poll that recovers a lost acknowledgement resends a data frame, so the mean exceeds 1 / (1 - P); it stays below the bound 1 / (1 - P)^2 at 5 of 5 rates. That excess is a property of linksim's protocol, not a defect of the closed form.

ns-3: the selective repeat of the ns-3 transfer runs over MAC retries, so its loss probability is not an input and the closed form cannot be applied to it; the ns-3 transmissions per segment are reported in `netsim/results/lrwpan_cells.csv` (tx_per_segment) for reference only.

## 7. Limits

- ns-3 is a simulator: its output is not a radio measurement, and its PHY error, propagation and CSMA models are those of the pinned version.
- The published figure is a prose range without settings; a cause that lands in the range is sufficient in simulation, not shown to be the cause in the paper.
- Hop count, node spacing, background traffic and the segment size are ASSUMPTIONS of this study.
- The paper's own transfer used CoAP from a repository (N6.2); it is not simulated.
- The lorawan module omits the 4 B message integrity code (N3.3) and models class A receive windows; linksim adds 13 B and models none.

_simulated (network) values are output of ns-3, not measurements_
